Research notes | Verified November 2, 2026

SCOPE AND EVIDENCE
A selected, sourced comparison. This is not a complete legislative inventory, a campaign-finance audit, or a voting recommendation. Candidate claims, proposals, endorsements, and government-recorded actions are separate.
Past enactment is not proof of measurable success, current implementation, or the absence of later litigation.

1. BACKGROUND AND QUALIFICATIONS

CONNIE CHAN
Party: Democratic
Current office: San Francisco District 1 Supervisor
Occupation: Supervisor; first elected in 2020
Education: UC Davis · bachelor’s degree; Galileo High School
Origin: Born in Hong Kong; moved to SF at 13
Experience: Community organizer, legislative aide; Budget Committee chair since 2023
Family and earlier career: Her mother studied at City College and worked as a claims processor at Chinese Hospital. Chan’s earlier work included Chinese-language interpretation and roles with Sophie Maxwell, Kamala Harris, and Aaron Peskin. Biography source: her campaign.
Sources: [2]

SCOTT WIENER
Party: Democratic
Current office: California State Senator · District 11
Occupation: State senator; first elected in 2016
Education: Duke · bachelor’s degree; Harvard Law School · law degree
Origin: Grew up in New Jersey; moved to SF in 1997
Experience: Attorney, SF deputy city attorney, former SF supervisor
Family and earlier career: The son of a small-business owner and a teacher, Wiener attended public school. He completed a judicial clerkship and spent a Fulbright year researching history in Chile. Biography source: his official Senate office.
Sources: [3]

4. CURRENT POLICY POSITIONS
Relationship labels below summarize the cited positions. Shared goals do not establish identical implementation.

HEALTHCARE | Shared direction
Connie Chan: Supports Medicare for All; also seeks restored Medicaid funding and extended ACA subsidies.
Scott Wiener: Supports Medicare for All; also seeks reversing Medicaid cuts and stronger patient protections.
Sources: [5]

WORK VISAS & GREEN CARDS | Shared direction
Connie Chan: Supports expansion, fair pay, worker protections, employer mobility, and clearing backlogs.
Scott Wiener: Supports expansion; specifies H-1B/O-1 processing reform and India/China backlog relief.
Sources: [5], [6]

HOUSING | Different approaches
Connie Chan: Emphasizes public and subsidized housing, vouchers, tenant protections, and repealing the Faircloth limit.
Scott Wiener: Emphasizes more construction, faster approvals, renter protections, and a national goal of 8 million homes.
Sources: [4]

AI SAFEGUARDS | Different approaches
Connie Chan: Supports federal rules, worker input, creator compensation, and independent review of high-risk uses.
Scott Wiener: Supports safety and transparency standards; authored California’s SB 53.
Sources: [4], [12]

DATA CENTERS | Different stance
Connie Chan: Supports a national moratorium until stronger protections exist for communities, utilities, and the environment.
Scott Wiener: Opposes a blanket moratorium; favors local decisions and energy/water protections.
Sources: [4]

FEDERAL WEALTH TAXES | Shared direction
Connie Chan: Supports higher taxes on fortunes above $50 million and a corporate minimum tax.
Scott Wiener: Supports Warren’s Ultramillionaire Tax: 2% above $50 million; 3% above $1 billion.
Sources: [4]

CALIFORNIA PROPOSITION 40 | Different stance
Connie Chan: Supports the state billionaire-tax measure.
Scott Wiener: Opposes the state billionaire-tax measure.
Sources: [17]

6. GARRY TAN, Y COMBINATOR, AND TECHNOLOGY BILLS
Garry Tan personally endorsed Scott Wiener for Congress. [9]
Y Combinator and Economic Security California Action were organizational sponsors of SB 1074. This is distinct from an organizational campaign endorsement. [10]
Tan opposed Wiener’s SB 1047 in 2024 and supported SB 1074 in 2026. His first-person account frames both stances around protecting startup competition. [16]
SB 1047 was vetoed. SB 53 was enacted. SB 1074 failed a committee vote; the tracker shows reconsideration granted with no later passage recorded when reviewed. [11–13]
